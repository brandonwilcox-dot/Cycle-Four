# 10 — Canon Rules

> The governing law of the IP. Three categories: what is **fixed** and
> may not be changed in any adaptation; what is **flexible** and may
> be freely reinterpreted; and what is **deliberately unanswered** and
> must be kept open. An adapter who honors only this section will
> still produce something that belongs to the property.

---

## Fixed — Inviolable Canon

These are load-bearing. Change one and it is no longer this IP.

**On the central truth.**

- The three factions are **one civilization, fractured.** Option B is
  the truth of the fiction. (Option A — three separate peoples — is
  what the factions believe, and it may be presented as true on the
  surface, but it is never the actual truth.)
- The three factions are **three categories of inheritance** of that
  one civilization — represented by the three substrates of the Mark:
  crystalline (Architect), biological fiber (Bloom), conductive mesh
  (Mesh).
- The recognition the IP delivers is *"you have been fighting
  yourself"* — and **the canon never states this sentence.** It is
  always assembled by the audience, never handed to them.
- The reveal is **structural and earned**, never a single sprung
  twist. Evidence is always present from the beginning; the
  conclusion is always withheld.

**On the cosmology.**

- The **convergence cycle** is real and recurring. This galaxy is the
  **fourth iteration**; three prior worlds reached the threshold and
  failed.
- The **Arrival** ends a failed convergence by leaving *silence* —
  worlds intact, all life simply gone. It is drawn specifically to
  *partial separation*: kin who became enemies and have not
  reconciled.
- **The Arrival never receives a face, a name, a form, a body, or a
  voice.** It is never depicted directly in any telling of this IP.
  The moment it is shown, it stops being the thing it is.
- The Ancients' **hypothesis is unconfirmed.** A chosen convergence
  *may* escape the Arrival; the canon never promises it does. Both
  branches of the hypothesis (convergence, or simply not being
  half-fractured at the threshold) are held open.

**On the Ancients.**

- The Ancients are **archivists** — librarians for the apocalypse.
  They preserve the record of civilizations they expect to fail.
- They are **tragic, not antagonistic.** They are **not omniscient.**
  They are **afraid.** They are never wise mentors, never gods, never
  villains.
- They **receive; they do not negotiate.** Their interventions are
  reactive — they answer dominance, countering whoever leads.
- The **seed vaults** (the "Ruins") are dormant, not ruined — intact
  and waiting. The **Mark** is a containment label. The **Pilgrimage**
  and the **Master Seed Vault** at the core are canon sites.

**On the factions.**

- Each faction has a real philosophy — refine / grow / connect — with
  a real strength and a real fatal flaw, and each faction's peak is
  the same shape: the philosophy succeeding so completely it no
  longer needs the people who held it.
- Each faction carries an **internal heresy** that is one step toward
  another faction (Architect Spiritual-Tech → Bloom; Bloom
  Assimilator → Mesh; Mesh Dreamer → the unbroken origin). These
  heresies are the seams of Option B and may not be removed.
- The factions **believe Option A.** Their certainty of separateness
  is itself canon.

**On structure.**

- There is **no villain, no chosen one, no prophecy.** The
  antagonist, to the extent there is one, is the fracture itself.
- The audience surrogate begins **faction-less and undefined**, and
  the deepest version of the story requires having been **more than
  one faction.**
- This telling **ends on the question.** The Arrival's actual impact
  is never depicted; the IP delivers the audience to the moment of
  understanding and stops.

## Flexible — Open to Reinterpretation

These may be freely changed, reskinned, expanded, or replaced to suit
a medium, an audience, or a story. None of them is load-bearing.

- **The title.** The property is titled *Cycle Four* — the Ancients'
  log designation for this iteration of the convergence. A specific
  adaptation in another medium may present it under a different name,
  but *Cycle Four* is the established title and the default.
- **All proper names** below the faction level: wave commanders
  (Cael, Vell, Yoss, FRAGMENT-NULL, etc.), star systems (Kerath-7,
  Orath, and the like), individual Ancient observers. These are
  illustrative, not fixed. New names in the same register are
  encouraged.
- **The faction names themselves** — "Architects," "Bloom," "Mesh" —
  may be re-rendered for a medium, *provided each faction's identity,
  philosophy, voice, and heresy survive intact.* The names are
  flexible; the cultures are not.
- **Sub-path names** ("Spiritual-Tech," "Assimilator," "Dreamer,"
  "Purist," "Networked," "Standard") may be renamed; the heresies
  they describe may not be removed.
- **The order, scale, and specifics of events** in the Fractured Age:
  particular battles, treaties, betrayals, alliances. The cascade and
  the diplomatic apparatus are canon; any individual incident is not.
- **Exact chronology.** The IP works in eras, not dates. An adapter
  may place events in time as a medium requires, provided the
  *order* in Section 7 holds.
- **The cadet's specifics.** Name, face, gender, background, voice —
  all open. The cadet's *trajectory* (faction-less → sorted → becomes
  all three) is fixed; everything else is the adapter's.
- **The medium and all of its mechanics.** Tier numbers, cooldowns,
  meters, interface — none of it is canon. (See Section 11.)
- **Tone calibration for audience.** A YA novel and a tabletop horror
  campaign may pitch the same canon very differently. The themes of
  Section 9 are fixed; their intensity is tunable.

## Deliberately Unanswered — Keep These Open

These are not gaps to be filled. They are load-bearing *absences*.
The IP is more powerful for not answering them, and an adaptation
that resolves them weakens the property. Leave them open.

| Open question | Why it must stay open |
|---|---|
| **What is the Arrival, actually?** | Named only obliquely; never shown. Its power is its unknowability. A defined Arrival is a defeated one. |
| **What drove the Ancients — what is "the next thing"?** | The fear beneath the watchers. It is the hook for whatever comes after. Naming it spends it. |
| **Is the hypothesis correct?** | The IP is the *question*. If the canon confirms that convergence works, there is no question left. |
| **What caused the Schism?** | Each faction's account contradicts the others. The contradiction characterizes the factions. A true answer would flatten all three. |
| **Who arrived in the galaxy first?** | All three factions claim it. The Ancients know and do not say. The claim matters more than the fact. |
| **The Ancients' own deepest origin.** | They are older than the cosmology's explanations. One thing must remain older than the answers. |
| **What happens after the threshold?** | Whether the fourth cycle breaks the pattern. This is the next story's to tell, if there is one. |

A useful test for any adaptation: if a draft *answers* one of these,
it has taken something out of the IP. The right move is almost always
to circle the question, deepen it, and walk away — exactly as the
Address itself does.
