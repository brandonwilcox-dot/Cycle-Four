# 7 — The Timeline

> A history of the IP — with one warning held over the whole section.
> This world works in **eras, not dates.** The civilizations involved
> are tens of thousands of cycles old; the watchers are older; and the
> canon deliberately refuses precise chronology. What follows is the
> *order* of things and the *shape* of the history. An adapter should
> never pin a year to any of it.
>
> The history also runs in **two strands** that an adapter must keep
> distinct: the history of *this galaxy* (the three factions), and the
> longer history of *the convergence cycle itself*, which spans other
> worlds entirely. They meet only when the Ancients arrive.

---

## Strand One — The Convergence Cycle Across Worlds

This strand is not the history of the three factions. It is the
history of the *pattern* — and of the watchers who have been
recording it.

### The three prior worlds

Long before this galaxy mattered to anyone, on three separate worlds,
three separate civilizations rose, fractured into variant expressions
of themselves, reached the convergence threshold, and failed it. Each
time, the Arrival came. Each time, the world went silent — intact,
emptied, recorded.

These three civilizations are unrelated to the three factions and
unrelated to each other. What links them is only the shape: a single
people, a fracture, a fractured age, a threshold, a failure. The
first two worlds fell and were archived. The **third** is the pivotal
one: its fall is the moment the Ancients finally understood that they
were not recording three tragedies but one *recurring law*.

### The Ancients' long watch

The Ancients are not native to any of these worlds. They are
something older, and at some point — for reasons the canon never
gives — they took on the work of archivists, moving from convergence
world to convergence world, documenting each as it approached its
threshold and preserving its record in seed vaults built in advance.

Their navigation records show **three prior departures**. They
archived the first world and moved on. They archived the second and
moved on. After the third — the one that taught them the pattern —
they did not merely move on. They went looking for a galaxy where the
cycle was *beginning again*, so that this time they could watch it
from the start. A surviving flight-log entry marks the decision:
*"The archive is complete for the third world. Beginning preparation
for the fourth."*

The fourth destination is this galaxy.

---

## Strand Two — The History of This Galaxy

### The deep past — the one civilization

Before the three factions, this galaxy held **one civilization**. The
canon is spare about it on purpose: it is the lost whole, and it is
more powerful as an absence than as a described thing. What is
certain is that it was a single people, and that it carried within it
the three potentials that would later become the Architects, the
Bloom, and the Mesh — three ways of being, not yet separated.

### The Schism

The pivotal event of the IP's history is the **Schism**: the fracture
of the one civilization into three diverging lineages.

**The cause of the Schism is deliberately unknown.** The canon does
not record whether it was catastrophe, ideology, distance, or the
simple slow drift of a people growing into incompatible shapes. Each
faction tells its own account, and the accounts contradict each other
in ways that reveal the faction more than the event. The Schism is a
question the IP keeps permanently open (see Section 10).

What *is* canon: the Schism produced three lineages, each of which,
over time, rewrote its own origin until it no longer remembered the
other two as kin. The fracture was not only physical. It was a
fracture of *memory* — and the forgetting is the part that matters.

### The Ancients' preparation

The Ancients arrived in this galaxy and recognized what they were
looking at: the early stages of the cycle they had already documented
three times. They began to prepare. They built seed vaults across the
galaxy and, at its heart, the Master Seed Vault. They built the
Pilgrimage site.

And they labeled what they built. The three-circle Mark on the
Pilgrimage floor is a containment stamp listing three categories of
material — and the Ancients placed it **before the three factions
existed as separate civilizations.** They did not need to wait for the
Schism to finish. They had seen the pattern three times. They knew a
single civilization would become three substrates, and they labeled
the vault accordingly, in advance, the way a scientist labels a
specimen jar before the specimen is inside it.

### The Fractured Age

Then came the long middle of the IP's history: the **Fractured Age**.
The three factions, now fully separate in their own minds, spread
across the galaxy. They warred. They traded. They built treaties and
broke them. They developed the whole apparatus of galactic
politics — alliances, betrayals, the careful etiquette of the cascade.
Each faction grew into its philosophy: the Architects into the
expedition, the Bloom into the living tide, the Mesh into the network.

The Fractured Age is *most of recorded history* and it is where most
stories set in this IP will take place. It is long enough, and stable
enough, that a civilization could live and die inside it believing the
three-faction galaxy was simply the way things had always been.

But the Age is not static. Across it, slowly, the seams show. The
internal heresies emerge — the Architect mystics, the Assimilator
strains, the Mesh Dreamers. The factions drift back, by inches,
toward one another even as they insist on their separateness. The
Fractured Age is not the absence of convergence. It is convergence
happening too slowly for anyone living inside it to see.

## The Present — The Fourth Cycle

The IP's "now" is the **convergence threshold** of the fourth cycle.

The three factions have expanded far enough, advanced far enough, and
leaked enough buried memory that they are once again *close enough to
reunify* — and they have not. They are in the dangerous condition:
partial separation, kin who became enemies, near enough to remember,
in the act of choosing.

This is the moment the Ancients crossed a galaxy to witness. It is
the moment the audience enters the story. Everything the IP depicts —
the war, the diplomacy, the journey inward toward the core, the slow
recovery of the Fragments — happens at the threshold.

The convergence cycle is also the reason the audience surrogate
exists in the form they do. A faction-less cadet who becomes, in
turn, every faction is a person living the whole threshold at once.

---

## The Approach

The final movement of the timeline is not an event the audience can
prevent. It is a direction.

Once the audience has assembled enough of the Fragments to understand
the question, the galaxy begins to change at its edges. The Outer
Arms start going **silent** — system by system, slowly, no faction
flags changing, no battles, just worlds going quiet one after another.
The silence has a vector. It is aimed inward. It is aimed here.

At the Pilgrimage, the celestial aperture — which has tracked a fixed
coordinate since before the factions had names — **shifts** for the
first time, to point at the spreading silence. It was always tracking
the Arrival. It simply was never close enough to be relevant until
now.

The Approach does not complete within the span of any telling of this
IP. The silence is slow; it will not reach the core inside the story
the audience is living. What the timeline delivers is not the
Arrival's impact but its *certainty*: the vector is unmistakable, the
watchers have stopped pretending otherwise, and the threshold the
factions are standing at has a deadline none of them set.

The IP ends with the timeline unfinished, on purpose. The history of
this galaxy runs right up to the question and stops. What comes after
the Approach — whether the fourth cycle breaks the pattern — is the
one piece of the timeline the canon refuses to write.
