# 2 — Cosmology

> The rules of the universe. What the convergence is, why it keeps
> happening, what waits at the end of it, and what the Ancients
> believe might escape it. This section is the load-bearing physics of
> the IP. Every other section rests on it.

---

## The Convergence Cycle

The central process of the cosmology is the **convergence cycle**. It
is not an event. It is a shape that civilizations fall into, and the
Ancients have watched enough of them to know the shape by heart.

A civilization begins unified. Over a long span — tens of thousands
of years — it fractures. Not by catastrophe, usually, but by
*divergence*: parts of it grow into ways of being so different that
they stop recognizing each other as kin. The corpus's three factions
are the model case. One people became three lineages — one that
refined technology toward pure efficiency, one that fused with
biology and the living world, one that dissolved the individual into
a network. Each lineage, given enough time, rewrites its own origin.
It does not remember the others as siblings. It remembers them as
rivals, or as enemies, or as things that were never relatives at all.

This is the fractured state. It is stable for a long time. The
lineages war, trade, ally, betray. They each believe their history.

Then comes the **convergence threshold**: the point at which the
fractured kin are once again *close enough to reunify* — close in
space, close in capability, close in the slow leak of buried memory —
**but have not done so.** This is the dangerous moment. Not the war.
Not the fracture. The specific condition of *partial separation*: kin
who have become enemies, who are near enough to remember what they
were, and who are in the act of choosing whether to.

The cycle resolves one of two ways. The kin recognize each other and
converge. Or they do not, and the threshold passes unanswered. The
Ancients have observed the second outcome three times. They have
never observed the first.

## The Four Iterations

The galaxy of this IP is the **fourth iteration** of the cycle the
Ancients have been documenting.

| Iteration | Status | What the Ancients know |
|---|---|---|
| First prior world | Fallen | Documented. Archived. Lost to silence. |
| Second prior world | Fallen | Documented. The Ancients' instrument readings of *its* Arrival survive as a record. |
| Third prior world | Fallen | The world whose fall made the Ancients finally understand the pattern. After it, they came here. |
| The fourth — this galaxy | In progress | The three factions. The experiment. Outcome unknown. |

The prior worlds are not this galaxy's history. They are *other
civilizations entirely*, in other places, that fell into the same
shape. The convergence cycle is not a local myth. It is something
closer to a law — a thing that happens to civilizations of a certain
kind, wherever they arise.

What is consistent across all four: a single civilization, a
fracture into variant expressions of itself, a long fractured age,
and a convergence threshold. What the Ancients are trying to learn
from the fourth is whether the threshold *has* to resolve the way the
first three did.

A sense of the timescales involved: one prior world ran for roughly
eighty thousand cycles between its fracture and its convergence
event. The oldest living memory the Bloom can reach — its deepest
inherited spore-memory — is about forty thousand cycles, and the
Ancient sites in this galaxy are *older than that*. The civilizations
in question are not young, and the watchers are older still. Deep
time is the native unit of this cosmology.

## The Arrival

When a convergence cycle fails — when the threshold passes and the
kin do not converge — something comes. The Ancients have no settled
name for it. Their records call it different things in different
places: *the condition*, *the arrival*, *the silence that follows*.
In the earliest records it is only a symbol that no translation
system can render. The corpus uses **the Arrival** as a working term.
Any telling of the IP should treat even that as provisional. The
thing is more frightening for being unnamed.

What it is, stated as plainly as the canon allows:

- **It is not a civilization.** It does not negotiate, communicate,
  or want.
- **It is not a weapon.** No one built it and aimed it.
- **It is not a predator.** It does not hunt or hunger in any
  biological sense.

The Ancients' best hypothesis, assembled across three observations
and three losses: a civilization at the convergence threshold emits a
**resonance** — a signature in the structure of space. The Arrival
reads that resonance not as an invitation but as a **vacancy**. The
specific shape of the signal says: *here is the space where a unified
civilization should be, and it is empty.* The Arrival fills
vacancies.

It does not conquer. It does not harvest. It moves through the space
a failed civilization occupied, and when it leaves, that space is
**silent**. Not destroyed — silent. No ruins, no bodies, no residue.
The worlds are physically intact. Everything that was alive in them
is simply, completely gone, and the absence has a texture the
Ancients' instruments can measure but cannot describe in any terms
the three factions would recognize.

The Arrival is drawn to one thing and one thing only: the resonance
of **partial separation**. It has no interest in a civilization that
never fractured. It has no interest in one that fractured so
completely it could never recognize a common origin. It is drawn to
kin who became enemies, who are close enough to remember, who are in
the act of choosing — and who choose wrong, or do not choose at all.

This is why the cosmology and the central mystery are the same thing.
The Arrival is not a separate danger bolted onto a story about a
fractured family. The Arrival *is* what makes the fracture matter. It
is the cost, made physical, of forgetting that you are kin.

## The Hypothesis — and Its Second Branch

The Ancients are archivists, not prophets. They do not know how to
stop the Arrival. But they have a hypothesis, and the three factions
are the experiment built to test it.

**The first branch — the hopeful one.** A civilization that
recognizes its own fracture and *chooses to converge* — that reaches
the threshold and reunifies — might produce a resonance the Arrival
cannot process. The Arrival evolved to consume the entropy of
collapse; a genuine, chosen convergence produces the opposite of
entropy. The Ancients believe this *might* be a signal the Arrival
cannot read as a vacancy. They have never seen it happen. They do not
know if they are right.

**The second branch — the colder one.** The deeper records carry an
unsettling refinement. The convergence event, on this reading,
occurs *whether the factions choose it or not*. The thing the
factions get to decide is only whether they are **present** for it.
Unified, or absent — the resonance is the same, and the Arrival
cannot distinguish between the two. On this reading, what saves a
civilization is not the warmth of reunion specifically. It is simply
*not being a half-fractured people standing at the threshold when the
moment comes.* Convergence is one way out. It may not be the only
one, and it may not be special.

The canon holds both branches at once and resolves neither. The
Ancients themselves do not know which is true. This uncertainty is
deliberate and must be preserved in any adaptation: the watchers are
not wise elders with the answer. They are frightened scholars with a
guess, who have prepared, in full, for the guess being wrong.

## What the Cosmology Is Not

A few firm exclusions, because the temptation to fill them will be
strong in adaptation:

- **There is no villain.** The Arrival has no will, no plan, no face,
  and must never acquire one. The factions are not evil; their
  conflict is structural. The Ancients are not antagonists; they are
  scared.
- **There is no prophecy.** Nothing is foretold. The Ancients have a
  pattern and a hypothesis. A pattern is not a destiny.
- **There is no chosen one.** The audience surrogate is not special.
  They are the experiment, which is a different and lonelier thing.
- **The cycle is not punishment.** Nothing did anything *wrong* to
  deserve the Arrival. Forgetting that you are kin is not a sin. It
  is just the most natural thing in the universe, and it is fatal.
