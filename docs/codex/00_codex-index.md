# The Codex — Canon Reference for Cycle Four

> The central canon reference for the IP. This document presents the
> world, characters, history, and cosmology as a **medium-agnostic
> canon** — the source material from which any expression of the IP
> can be built: this game, a tabletop game a game master could run, a
> graphic novel, a YA novel, or anything else.
>
> The title of the property is **Cycle Four** — the Ancients' own log
> designation for this iteration of the convergence ("Cycle four.
> Subjects present. Observation active."). The Codex otherwise refers to
> the setting in medium-neutral terms — "the galaxy," "the world," "the
> convergence" — so the canon reads cleanly in any medium.

---

## What the Codex Is

The design corpus (`core/10`–`core/22`) is thirteen interlocking
documents that interleave **lore** with **game mechanics**. That is
the correct shape for building *the game*. It is the wrong shape for
building *anything else*.

The Codex extracts the canon — the parts that are true about the
world regardless of medium — and organizes it so that someone who
will never read a design document can pick it up and tell a story in
this universe. A novelist does not need to know the production
cooldown of a Tier 4 unit. A game master does not need the Dominance
Meter's threshold percentages. They need the world: who these people
are, what happened to them, what is coming, and what it all means.

This is the formal expression of the project's founding philosophy:
**the IP is the asset, not the specific game implementation.**

## The Canon / Design Boundary

The Codex holds **canon**. Game mechanics live in the design corpus
and are touched here only in Section 11, Adaptation Notes.

| Belongs in the Codex (canon) | Belongs in the design corpus (mechanics) |
|---|---|
| The three factions are one fractured civilization | How sub-paths gate unit modifier slots |
| The Ancients archive every civilization they watch fall | The Dominance Meter's four thresholds |
| The Arrival ends a failed convergence in silence | Wave composition by tier number |
| The Mark labels what the seed vault contains | The Mark's brightness scale of 0–100 |
| Wave commanders are the voice of the enemy civilization | The 24-commander tier 11–25 dialogue table |

When canon and mechanics genuinely fuse — as they do in the Memory
Tier reveal structure — the Codex describes the *narrative truth* and
defers the *delivery method* to Adaptation Notes.

## The Spine, Stated Once

Everything in the Codex resolves to a single structural truth, and
every adapter should hold it from the first page:

**The three factions believe they were always separate. They are
wrong. They are one civilization, fractured — and the story is the
slow, earned recognition that the war was always a family tearing
itself apart.**

The corpus names the two readings *Option A* (peer factions, the
truth of the surface) and *Option B* (fractured kin, the truth of the
fiction). The audience begins inside Option A and is delivered,
without ever being told outright, to Option B.

The single sentence the canon is built to make the audience say —
and which the canon itself must never say first — is:

*You have been fighting yourself.*

## Provenance

The Codex is derived from the design corpus authored across eleven
design sessions: `core/10_faction-lore.md` through
`core/22_interface-design.md`, and the founding `Cowork_Faction_Lore_Brief.md`.
Where the Codex and the corpus disagree, the corpus is the older
record and the Codex is the reconciled one; discrepancies should be
resolved in favor of the Codex and the corpus updated to match.
