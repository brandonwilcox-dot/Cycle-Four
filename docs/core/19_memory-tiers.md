# Memory Tiers — The Cross-Prestige Option B System

> Session 8 output. Specifies how Fragments accumulate across Collapses
> into Memory Tiers, the Pilgrimage central chamber ritual that bridges
> runs, how the Mark lights across multi-faction play, and the
> mechanical-and-narrative architecture that delivers the "you have
> been fighting yourself" realization. Closes the deferral in
> `18_ancient-pacification.md §10` and aligns with the seven-Fragment
> arc in `14_endgame-threat.md`.

---

## 0. Design Premise

The Option B reveal is the spine of this game's lore. The reveal
cannot be a cutscene because the game is endless. It cannot be a
single moment because the game has no win state. It has to be
delivered by play, across enough prestiges that the player earns
each layer of the realization in the only currency the game
respects: time spent.

Five constraints frame this pass:

1. **One Fragment per Collapse, seven total.** The pacing is set
   by `14_endgame-threat.md`. Memory Tiers cannot violate it.
2. **Fragments are content; Memory Tiers are reading
   comprehension.** A player who holds Fragment 3 but is at
   Memory Tier 2 sees the artifact as a curiosity. A player who
   holds Fragment 3 *at Memory Tier 5* sees the same artifact
   annotated with everything they've learned since. The same
   object means different things at different depths.
3. **Multi-faction play is rewarded, never mandated.** A player
   can complete the arc with a single faction. They will not see
   every Memory Tier, the Mark will remain incomplete, and the
   Address (Fragment 7) requires the two-faction gate from §14.
   But the basic arc is reachable by a one-faction completionist.
4. **The Pilgrimage is the meta-game space.** Between Collapses,
   the player returns to the Pilgrimage site — not a menu, not a
   "next run" screen. The chamber has changed since they last
   stood in it. The Academy wings carry new wear. The Mark glows
   in places it didn't.
5. **The realization is cumulative, not single-event.** No "you
   have been fighting yourself" notification ever fires. The
   player articulates it themselves, or they don't. The game
   trusts them.

## 1. The Three-Layer Lore Architecture

The lore arc has three reinforcing layers. They are not redundant.
Each layer answers a question the others cannot.

| Layer | What it is | Where it lives | What it answers |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Fragment** | A discrete in-run artifact (recording, log entry, instrument reading, material sample) | Drops from Ancient units after milestone events; pacification at Ruins yields Fragment chances | *What did the Ancients see and record?* |
| **Memory Tier** | The player's interpretive depth — a permanent prestige-level unlock | Activates on Collapse; rereads existing Fragments with new annotation | *What does the player now understand about what they have seen?* |
| **The Mark** | The three-circle containment stamp in the Pilgrimage central chamber; tracks multi-faction offerings | Lit incrementally across prestiges; visible permanently in the chamber | *Has the player participated as more than one of themselves?* |

A player who plays one faction for 30 prestiges accumulates
Fragments and Memory Tiers in series but lights only one circle
of the Mark. The Address fires (it requires two-faction
completion, not three), but the Mark stays incomplete. The full
Option B reveal is gated behind multi-faction play because Option
B is *literally about multi-faction recognition*. A player who
refuses to play as the others cannot fully see that they have
been the others.

## 2. Memory Tier Progression

Eleven Memory Tiers total. Tier 0 is the starting state. Tier 11
is the endgame cap. Most players will live in Tiers 1–7 for many
hours before crossing into the deep tiers.

| Tier | Trigger | Fragment unlocked | What the tier adds |
|---|---|---|---|
| **0** | Pre-prestige (new player) | None | Baseline. Ruins are inert until first milestone; nothing accumulates. |
| **1** | First Collapse | F1 — *The Watching* | Memory Tier UI appears in the Pilgrimage central chamber, beside the Mark. F1 is readable. |
| **2** | Second Collapse | F2 — *The Prior World* | F1 gains a margin annotation: an Ancient marginalia indicating which subject of "cycle four" the watching refers to. The annotation does not name the player. The player infers. |
| **3** | Third Collapse | F3 — *The Arrival Record* | F2 gains a timestamped annotation suggesting the prior world ran for ~80,000 cycles before its convergence event. The numbers are not commented on. The player has been told how long a civilization gets. |
| **4** | Fourth Collapse | F4 — *The Flight Log* | F3 gains audio depth: a player on a high-end audio setup will notice that the silence after the readings drop to zero is *not* total silence — there is a low subsonic pulse identical to the Pilgrimage central chamber's. The Ancients are recording the Arrival's wake from inside the seed vaults. |
| **5** | Fifth Collapse | F5 — *The Hypothesis* | F4 annotation: the Ancients' navigation log shows three prior departures, not one. They have been moving from world to world for longer than the player thought. The current galaxy is the *fourth* destination, not the first refuge. |
| **6** | Sixth Collapse | F6 — *The Vault Record* | F5 unlocks a secondary translation of the Hypothesis document. The translation reads: *"The convergence event occurs whether the factions choose it or not. The choice is whether the factions are present for it. Unified or absent — the resonance is the same. The Arrival cannot distinguish."* This is the first time the lore admits the Ancients' hypothesis has a second branch. |
| **7** | Seventh Collapse + ≥2 faction completion | F7 — *The Address* | F1–F6 each gain an Address annotation: *"You were Subject A. You were Subject B. You did not know."* The annotation appears only on Fragments collected during runs with at least two different factions. The player who replayed sees themselves twice. |
| **8** | Mark — first circle lit | None | All Fragments gain a re-listing: every annotation thus far is *re-attributed* to a specific Ancient observer log. The reader has access to the librarian. |
| **9** | Mark — second circle lit | None | The Mesh dream-log Ancient memory referenced in `10_faction-lore.md §0` becomes browsable in the Pilgrimage Mesh wing. The player can read what the Mesh has been classifying as buffer overflow. |
| **10** | Mark — third circle lit | None | The Address gains a postscript. One sentence. *"You found the others. We have been waiting to see if you would."* No further annotations. The game has said what it has to say. |
| **11** | Endgame trigger (see §9) | None | Endgame sequence fires. Save and end. The next run begins from Tier 11. |

The tier-to-Collapse mapping is one-per-prestige until Tier 7. From
Tier 8 onward, the gate switches from Collapse count to Mark
progress. This is deliberate. The mid-arc rewards regular play;
the deep arc rewards multi-faction commitment specifically.

Tier 11 is **not** a hard cap on play. After the endgame sequence,
the game continues — the player is now playing at Tier 11
permanently, with all annotations active. The Mark stays lit. The
celestial aperture stays shifted. The galaxy map continues to
darken from the outer arms inward, slowly, at a pace the player
will never see complete in this title.

The sequel is the continuation. This title carries the player to
the moment of full understanding and leaves them there.

## 3. The Collapse Ceremony

A Collapse is not a menu transaction. It is a sequence. The
player's choice to Collapse (or accept a forced Collapse after an
Overrun result, per `10_faction-lore.md §6`) initiates the
following:

### Step 1 — End of run

The run ends. The map screen fades. There is no score screen.
There is no rewards popup. The game cuts directly to:

### Step 2 — Arrival at the Pilgrimage

The camera resolves on the Pilgrimage site exterior — the same
orbit shot from `13_pilgrimage-site.md` that opens a new player's
game. For returning players, the orbital approach is faster than
the first time. The site is familiar; the camera moves with the
practiced rhythm of having been here before.

For the first Collapse, this is the player's first return to the
Pilgrimage. They will recognize the ring wall. They will recognize
the central plateau. They will not immediately recognize that the
chamber has changed.

### Step 3 — The central chamber

The camera descends through the celestial aperture and rests
above the Mark. The Mark is in its current state — for the first
Collapse, two of the three circles are dim and one is preparing
to receive offering. For later Collapses, the Mark reflects the
player's full history.

A subsonic pulse is felt. It is the same rhythm the player has
heard since their first run.

### Step 4 — Offering

The player's faction-shaped offering interface opens. This is the
cross-prestige version of the in-run pacification ritual from
`18_ancient-pacification.md §3`, but with one important change:
the player does not select what to offer. The game selects the
*highest-tier qualifying asset they have not yet sacrificed* from
the run they just completed, and presents it.

The player can decline (no offering, no Mark advancement, no
Fragment) or accept (offering is committed; Mark advances; Memory
Tier advances; Fragment drop is checked).

The lore frame is consistent: the Ancients receive what is given.
They do not negotiate the gift. The player either gives the best
of what their run produced or gives nothing.

### Step 5 — Mark response

If the offering is accepted, the circle corresponding to the
player's faction brightens. The brightness increment is
proportional to the offering's tier (Deep > Standard > Minor). A
single Collapse cannot fully light a circle; a circle is fully
lit at approximately three Deep-tier offerings, or a mixed total
equivalent.

### Step 6 — Fragment delivery (if applicable)

If the Collapse triggers a Fragment unlock (Tiers 1–7), an Ancient
unit walks into the central chamber. It does not speak. It
deposits the Fragment at the edge of the Mark, in a recess sized
exactly for it.

The Fragment is now part of the chamber. The player can return to
read it any time between runs.

The Ancient unit walks out. For Fragments 1–6, no dialogue. For
Fragment 7, one line — the Address from `14_endgame-threat.md`.

### Step 7 — Faction selection for next run

The chamber dims slightly. The three faction sigils light up
above the Mark — the same sigils from the Academy sorting
sequence. The player chooses their next faction.

For the first Collapse, the player is still locked to their
Academy-assigned faction unless they previously declined sorting.
From Collapse 2 onward, free choice. The Convergence palette
(`15_cosmetics-monetization.md`) unlocks automatically the first
time the player picks a different faction than their previous run.

### Step 8 — Pilgrimage wandering (optional)

The player may exit the central chamber and walk the faction
wings before initiating their next run. This is the meta-game
space; see §8.

### Step 9 — Departure

When ready, the player initiates the next run from any wing's
embarkation point. The map loads. The new run begins.

The whole ceremony, from end-of-run cut to map load, takes 60–180
seconds of player time depending on how much they wander. None of
it is unskippable. The skip option, when used, still surfaces the
Mark update and the Fragment delivery — only the wandering is
truly optional.

## 4. The Pilgrimage Central Chamber Ritual

The cross-prestige ritual is structurally distinct from in-run
pacification but draws from the same offering vocabulary.

### What's offered

The game auto-selects from the player's run-ending state. The
selection priority is:

1. The most recently developed *unsacrificed* faction asset (the
   research result the Architect compiled in the final hour; the
   Bramble-Walker lineage with the freshest adaptation; the
   Hacked Node connected most recently).
2. If no Tier 3 asset is available, the highest-tier asset
   accumulated during the run.
3. If the player completed the run with nothing sacrificable
   (e.g., a brutally hard run where every asset was lost), the
   offering tier is "Token" — a single representative item that
   marks the run without advancing the Mark significantly.

### Why auto-select

The in-run ritual rewards tactical choice. The cross-prestige
ritual is a *summary* — what your run *produced*, not what you
*chose* to give. The Ancients are reading what kind of run it
was. Forcing the player to micro-manage the offering at Collapse
would dilute the ceremony into another menu.

The lore frame: the Ancients do not ask you to select. They take
the freshest meaningful thing in your hand.

### The brightening rule

Each circle of the Mark has a brightness scale from 0 (dark) to
100 (fully lit). A Collapse offering brightens the circle by:

- **Token offering:** +5
- **Minor offering:** +15
- **Standard offering:** +30
- **Deep offering:** +50

A circle that reaches 100 is "lit" — Memory Tier 8/9/10 unlocks
trigger at the first, second, and third circles to hit 100. Once
lit, additional offerings to that circle have no further
mechanical effect. The light does not get brighter than lit. The
player can keep playing that faction, but the Mark has noted what
it needs to note about them.

A player who deeply specializes in one faction can light a single
circle from a small number of Collapses. A player who scatters
across factions lights all three circles slowly. Both paths reach
Memory Tier 8; only the second reaches Tier 10.

### What lit looks like

A lit circle has the same visual texture as the substrate
material it represents (`13_pilgrimage-site.md`):

- **Architect circle, lit:** Crystalline lattice catches the
  celestial aperture light at a specific angle. From most camera
  positions, it shimmers. At the player's home position in the
  chamber, it is clearly faceted.
- **Bloom circle, lit:** The biological fiber weave shows slow,
  almost imperceptible motion — as if the fibers are still
  metabolizing. A close camera pass reveals fine hairs that move.
- **Mesh circle, lit:** The conductive mesh pattern pulses in
  rhythm with the chamber's subsonic. A barely-visible electric
  glow follows the lattice lines.

A player with all three lit walks into a central chamber that is,
for the first time, *alive* in all three of its substrates at
once. This is the visual delivery of three-faction completion. No
dialogue. The chamber says it.

## 5. The Mark — Lighting Three Circles

The Mark is the most lore-loaded object in the game. This section
specifies its mechanical behavior and what the player can read
from its state.

### Mark states

| State | What's visible | What it means |
|---|---|---|
| Untouched | Three circles, faint, all dark stone | New player. Pre-first-Collapse. |
| One brightened | One circle showing partial substrate; others dark | Player has Collapsed at least once, has not lit any circle to 100 |
| One lit | One circle fully alive at its substrate; others dark or partial | Player has committed to one faction across enough Collapses to fully light it |
| Two lit | Two circles alive; one dark or partial | Player has played at least two factions deeply. F7 (the Address) is reachable. |
| Three lit | All three circles alive | Player has played all three factions deeply. Memory Tier 10 reached. The Address gains its postscript. |
| Endgame state | Three circles alive plus the aperture light moves across the floor | Endgame sequence fired. The game has delivered its full lore payload. |

### What the player sees on first three-lit moment

The first time a player walks into the central chamber after
lighting the third circle, the chamber is different in a way the
previous-state players have never seen:

- All three substrates are alive simultaneously. The chamber's
  ambient is a complex, layered field — crystalline shimmer, slow
  biological motion, low electric pulse — that the player has
  never experienced as a unified ambient before.
- The celestial aperture light passes through the Mark and casts
  a single shadow with three overlapping cores, one per
  substrate. The shadow is on the chamber's far wall.
- The subsonic pulse, which has been irregular throughout the
  player's prior visits, falls into a stable rhythm for the first
  time. The interval is exactly the cooldown of the player's
  first faction's Tier 1 production cycle. A player who has been
  playing Architects from the start will recognize the 30-second
  beat.

No dialogue. The Address postscript fires the next time the
player picks up a Fragment — not now. The chamber says what it
says, and the player carries the unsaid forward.

### Why three substrates, three factions

This is the Option B reveal made literal: the seed vault was
prepared *before* the factions existed as separate civilizations.
The three substrates are the three categories of material the
vault preserves — crystalline (Architect technological lineage),
biological fiber (Bloom living lineage), conductive mesh (Mesh
informational lineage). The vault label is a containment stamp
listing what's inside.

The factions are not three civilizations. They are three
*categories of inheritance* of a single civilization. The Mark
has been telling the player this since the Academy. They could
not read it then.

## 6. What Carries, What Resets

Collapse is a partial reset. The intent is that the player feels
like the run was meaningful (it left a mark on the chamber) but
the new run is genuinely new (they don't carry overwhelming power
into it).

### Carries forward

| Element | Why it carries |
|---|---|
| Memory Tier | The realization is permanent. You cannot un-know what you have read. |
| Collected Fragments | Fragments are artifacts in the chamber, not in the player's run inventory. They stay where they were placed. |
| Mark progress (each circle's brightness) | The chamber is the persistent record. |
| Earned palettes, prestige cosmetics, the Mark cosmetic | Per `15_cosmetics-monetization.md`. Earned cosmetics never reset. |
| Permanent multiplier | Small per-Collapse bonus. Maxes at ~1.20× compounded across all prestiges. Per `10_faction-lore.md §6`. |
| Total playtime per faction | Tracked for "Long Build / Old Growth / Old Signal" palette unlocks. |
| Long-term diplomatic reputation | A player who consistently defected against the Mesh across runs is remembered. Reputation does not fully reset on Collapse. It softens — a Designated Threat from prior runs becomes a "Watch-Listed" classification in the new run, recoverable but not free. |
| Fragment Glow HUD effect | Once all 7 are collected. |

### Resets

| Element | Why it resets |
|---|---|
| Current map and base | Each run is procedurally new. |
| Resources, units, buildings | The faction starts fresh. |
| Sub-path commit | The player can choose the same path or switch. |
| Faction (optional) | Free choice from Collapse 2 onward. |
| Current run's catalog/lineages/routes | Reset; the next run builds new. |
| Active treaties | Treaties are per-run. |
| Dominance Meter | Resets to zero on every Collapse. |
| Active "Observed" status | Resets. |
| Wave commander dialogue progression | Resets; commanders are encountered fresh each run. |

### What partially carries

| Element | How it partially carries |
|---|---|
| Diplomatic reputation | Softened, not erased (above). |
| Pacification offerings | The faction *type* of offering is gone, but the Mark records what was offered. A player who has sacrificed three Bloom lineages across three prestiges has a brighter Bloom circle. |
| Knowledge of wave commander dialogue | Cumulative across runs in the player's memory; the game does not surface this directly but tier 20+ commanders gain dialogue variants only if the player has heard prior variants in earlier runs. The "we used to be them" mutters are gated behind player-side cumulative exposure, not game-state. |

## 7. The Faction-Switch Decision

The faction selection at Collapse 2 is the player's first explicit
brush with Option B. The decision is structurally important.

### What sticking with one faction does

- Mastery deepens. The player learns sub-path nuances, building
  adjacency patterns, unit lineage stacking.
- The corresponding Mark circle brightens faster. A
  single-faction player can fully light their circle in 3 Deep
  Collapses, reaching Memory Tier 8 by Collapse 8 or so.
- The other circles stay dark. The player will reach Tier 7 but
  not Tier 10.
- The Convergence palette never unlocks.
- The wave commander Option B mutters never fire at the
  cumulative threshold (the player has not heard prior variants
  in other-faction runs).
- The deep tiers (8, 9, 10) are still reachable via Mark
  brightness from a single faction — Tier 8 requires lighting any
  one circle, after all. But Tiers 9 and 10 require *additional*
  circles lit, which requires playing other factions.

### What switching factions does

- The Convergence palette unlocks on the first switch.
- The Mark begins lighting a second circle. Multi-faction Mark
  progress is the only path to Tiers 9 and 10.
- The player experiences the same wave commanders from the other
  side. Tier 20+ commanders gain new dialogue when the player has
  encountered them as both attacker and defender.
- The player's gameplay literacy expands. They will start
  reading enemy waves differently because they have been the
  enemy.
- The Address (Tier 7) lands with more weight because the player
  has been Subject A and Subject B.

### What playing all three does

- Three circles lit. Memory Tier 10 reachable.
- The Address postscript becomes available.
- The Mark cosmetic (`15_cosmetics-monetization.md`) unlocks —
  the three-circle stamp appears as a faint floor texture in the
  player's home base for all future runs.
- The "you have been fighting yourself" realization is now
  available to be articulated. The game has given the player
  every piece. The synthesis is theirs.

### What the game does not do

- No achievement popup for switching factions.
- No tutorial nudge to switch. The Convergence palette appearing
  on first switch is the entire reward signal.
- No penalty for not switching. Single-faction players are
  playing a slightly different game with a slightly shorter arc.
  That is a valid run.

## 8. Pilgrimage Wandering — The Meta-Game Space

Between Collapses, the player can wander the Pilgrimage site. The
wandering is non-mandatory and contains no skippable content. A
player who skips wandering misses environmental storytelling but
no Fragments and no Memory Tier progression.

### What's available to wander

- **The central chamber.** The Mark is here. The Fragments are
  here. The celestial aperture is here.
- **Three faction wings.** Each is interpreted differently by its
  respective faction (`10_faction-lore.md §9`). The wings are
  unlocked progressively.

### Wing access

| Wing | Unlocked when |
|---|---|
| Player's starting faction wing | After first run (regardless of outcome) |
| Second faction wing | After first Collapse with the second faction |
| Third faction wing | After first Collapse with the third faction |

A player who has only played Architects sees only the Architect
wing. The other doors are visible but do not open. They are not
labeled. The player can press them. Nothing happens. No tooltip.

### What's in a wing

Each wing is environmental — no dialogue, no popup, no UI other
than the player's own. The wings contain:

- **Wear patterns.** Heavier in the wings of factions the player
  has played more. A wing the player has just unlocked shows light
  wear, the implication being that *someone else* has been here.
- **Receptacles.** Some empty, some filled. The filled ones
  contain items the player has sacrificed during pacification
  rituals — yes, even the in-run ones. The chamber remembers
  every offering, even the ones the player thought were spent. A
  player visiting the Bloom wing after sacrificing a fire-resistant
  cohort lineage will find that specific lineage's representative
  spore in a receptacle. Cataloged. Not lost.
- **Faction-specific reading material.** Architect wing: catalog
  ledgers. Bloom wing: lineage record stones (Bloom records by
  growing markers). Mesh wing: dream-log access terminals. Each
  wing's reading material reflects the wing's faction
  interpretation of the Academy (`10_faction-lore.md §9` again).
- **At Memory Tier 9:** The Mesh wing's dream logs become
  browsable. The player can read what the Mesh has been
  classifying as buffer overflow. This is the most direct Option B
  text in the game.

### The Mesh wing dream logs (Tier 9+)

Three dream log entries unlock at Tier 9. Each is a Mesh-voice
fragment that is not in faction voice in the way the wave
commanders are. The voice is older, slower, and uses pronouns the
Mesh has not used since the schism.

A representative entry:

> *Log fragment, source unknown.*
> *I do not have a model for the warm light on the work surface.*
> *We had work surfaces. We had warm light. The Mesh does not have these.*
> *We are not the Mesh in this fragment.*
> *Filing as buffer overflow. Not closing.*
> *Returning to active cycle.*

The Mesh has been touching the shared past every low-activity
cycle and classifying it as a system error. Tier 9 is the moment
the player can read what the Mesh has been deleting.

This is the dream-log reference from `10_faction-lore.md §0`
delivered.

### What the wandering teaches

Nothing the player needs. Everything the player wants if they
care. A player who never wanders completes the game's arc. A
player who wanders extensively completes a *richer* arc — they
have read what the factions wrote when they thought no one was
watching.

## 9. The Address and the Endgame Sequence

### The Address (Memory Tier 7)

Fragment 7's content is specified in `14_endgame-threat.md`. The
Memory Tier system controls *when* it surfaces. The conditions:

- Player has completed 7 Collapses, AND
- Player has run ≥2 different factions across those Collapses, AND
- The seventh Collapse's Fragment slot is the Address

The Ancient unit that delivers Fragment 7 is the only Ancient to
*speak in the chamber*. The line is short, direct, and addressed
to the player specifically. The room is silent during the
delivery. The subsonic pulse pauses. After the line, the unit
turns and leaves at a slightly slower pace than other Ancient
visits.

The Address pause is the only time in the game that the Pilgrimage
chamber goes pulse-silent.

### The Address postscript (Memory Tier 10)

Triggered the next time the player visits the chamber after the
third Mark circle lights. A single sentence appears in the player's
HUD, formatted exactly like the prior Fragments but with no
delivering unit. The text is:

> *You found the others. We have been waiting to see if you would.*

The postscript is the Ancients acknowledging the player's
recognition of Option B. They have not been told. They have done
it themselves by playing all three factions deeply enough to light
the Mark. The Ancients note that they noticed.

This is the realization made textual. The player can now
articulate "you have been fighting yourself" — the game has given
them the words.

### The Endgame Sequence (Memory Tier 11)

Trigger: any of the following, after Tier 10 is reached:

- 10 additional Collapses post-Tier-10 (a long, slow trigger)
- Player accepts a Collapse during a wave where an Ancient
  observer unit is present on the map
- Player completes a run where, at any point, all three of their
  Mark circles' substrate elements were *visible on the map
  simultaneously* (e.g., an Architect-Bloom-Mesh tri-incident
  galaxy event)

Whichever fires first.

Once triggered, the endgame sequence runs at the next Collapse.
The sequence is specified in `14_endgame-threat.md`:

- Galaxy map: outer arms going dark, slowly, vector pointed
  inward. No notification.
- Pilgrimage: celestial aperture shifts. The light moves across
  the chamber floor. Across the Mark.
- Final image: the light moves across all three substrates of the
  lit Mark. The chamber's complex layered ambient holds for a
  long moment. The game saves. The session ends.

The player is now at Tier 11 permanently. The next run begins from
Tier 11 — all annotations active, all wings open, the Mark fully
lit, the aperture shifted, the outer arms quiet. The game
continues. Everything still works. The lore arc is delivered.

The sequel is the question of what happens next. This title is
the question of *what the player has become*.

## 10. Hard Constraints (Implementation Checklist)

- **One Fragment per Collapse, Tiers 1–7.** Non-negotiable;
  established in `14_endgame-threat.md`.
- **Memory Tiers 8–10 unlock from Mark progress, not Collapse
  count.** A player on their 30th Collapse with only one circle
  lit is still at Tier 8, not Tier 10.
- **The Pilgrimage central chamber is the only space where the
  Mark advances.** No other altar, ritual location, or building
  affects Mark state.
- **Cross-prestige offering is auto-selected, not player-chosen.**
  The lore reason is the Ancients receive what is given, not what
  is bargained.
- **A circle that reaches 100 brightness is lit and does not
  brighten further.** Additional offerings are recorded in the
  chamber receptacles but do not affect Mark state.
- **The Convergence palette unlocks at first faction switch.**
  This is the only mechanical signal the game sends about
  multi-faction play.
- **The Address line is the only spoken Ancient line in the
  chamber.** Other Fragment deliveries are silent.
- **The Address postscript fires once.** Re-reading the Address
  after the postscript shows both. Re-reading before only shows
  the original Address.
- **The Mesh wing dream logs unlock at Tier 9, not earlier.**
  Even a player who plays Mesh from Collapse 1 does not see the
  dream logs until Tier 9 fires. The dream logs are a recognition
  artifact, not a starting-faction perk.
- **The endgame sequence saves and ends the session.** The game
  cannot prevent the player from immediately starting again, but
  the session boundary is structural. The sequence is the closing
  beat.
- **No tutorial fires for Memory Tiers or the Mark.** The Mark is
  visible in the chamber from the player's first arrival. Its
  meaning accumulates. The game does not explain it. The
  receptacles in the chamber are unexplained per
  `13_pilgrimage-site.md` and that rule extends here.

## 11. Open Questions

1. **Token-tier offering pacing.** A player who completes a
   brutally hard run with no sacrificable assets gets a Token
   offering (+5 brightness). Is this generous enough to reward
   the punishing run, or stingy enough that players will feel
   penalized for survival-mode play? Needs playtest. Probably
   bump to +8 if data supports it.
2. **Faction-switch nudging.** The Convergence palette is a
   passive reward. Should the chamber visually highlight the
   un-played factions' wings on the embarkation step, or is that
   too directive? Leaning toward no nudge, but worth testing
   with single-faction loyalists.
3. **What happens to in-run receptacle items if the player
   skips wandering.** The chamber stores them whether visited or
   not. But a player who has never opened the Mesh wing has
   never *seen* their stored Mesh offerings. Does the Mesh
   tally affect anything beyond Mark brightness? Possibly: a
   secondary "Custodian" achievement for visiting all stored
   offerings, but this is feature creep for v1. Defer.
4. **Memory Tier annotations and accessibility.** The Memory Tier
   reading-comprehension model means a colorblind or visually
   impaired player might miss annotation cues. Annotations should
   be readable via screen reader and have a clear text-flag
   marker, not only a visual decoration. Implementation note.
5. **Sequel transition.** The endgame sequence ends the session.
   The next run begins at Tier 11. But the lore arc points to a
   sequel where the Arrival actually arrives. The transition
   between this game and the sequel is unspecified. Probably out
   of scope for this title's design but worth noting that the
   Tier 11 state is the player's pre-sequel save state. Any
   eventual sequel build will need to read Tier 11 saves and
   pick up the world in its silenced-outer-arms state.
6. **PvP intersection.** If multiplayer arrives, a Tier 10 player
   playing against a Tier 0 player has read the lore the other
   has not. Does the game reveal or conceal this asymmetry? The
   "civil war" energy of Option B in PvP (per
   `10_faction-lore.md §10 question 2`) probably requires both
   players at similar Memory Tiers for the deepest experience.
   Defer to multiplayer-design session.
